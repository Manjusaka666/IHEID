# Speaker Notes: Changing Global Linkages

## Slide 1. Title
Today I present Gopinath, Gourinchas, Presbitero, and Topalova's "Changing Global Linkages: A New Cold War?" I focus on the paper's economic logic: what is fragmenting, why aggregate trade misses it, and how connector countries change the meaning of decoupling.

## Slide 2. Argument in One Sentence
The paper does not claim that globalization has collapsed. It argues that global linkages are being rewired. Trade, FDI, and portfolio positions increasingly sort along geopolitical lines after 2022. The distinctive point is that nonaligned economies can preserve indirect links between rival blocs.

## Slide 3. Presentation Structure
I organize the presentation around the paper's logic. I start from the aggregate fact, then move to bloc measurement and reallocation. I then discuss the gravity model and the full Table 1 results. The second half compares the current episode with the Cold War, explains connector countries, and closes with methodological strengths, limits, and significance.

## Slide 4. Aggregate Trade Has Not Collapsed
This figure blocks a simple deglobalization story. World trade remains high relative to GDP. The Cold War period also shows that global trade can rise even when politically distant pairs trade less with each other. The right object is therefore not aggregate openness. It is the geography of bilateral links.

## Slide 5. Relative Cross Bloc Slowdown
The current period shows a relative split. Trade growth is lower between blocs than within blocs after the Ukraine war. This is the empirical motivation for comparing aligned and politically distant country pairs rather than looking only at world totals.

## Slide 6. Data Used by the Paper
The paper combines goods trade, announced greenfield FDI projects, and portfolio holdings. These are different types of global linkage. The value of the design is that it can ask whether the same geopolitical pattern appears in current trade, forward looking investment, and financial portfolios.

## Slide 7. Bloc Classification
The bloc definition is central. The wider classification uses ideal point distance from the United States and China. The narrower classification uses a more conservative assignment. Nonaligned economies are not residual noise. They are analytically important because they may connect the rival blocs.

## Slide 8. Import and FDI Source Reallocation
The Modified Lilien Index rises after 2022. That means partner composition changes even when aggregate trade does not collapse. The figure tells us that economies are reallocating across import and FDI sources, which is exactly the kind of movement that aggregate openness hides.

## Slide 9. FDI Source Reallocation
The FDI pattern has similar timing. This matters because announced investment projects reflect expectations about future production locations. Firms appear to be reconsidering where production should take place, not only where final goods are shipped today.

## Slide 10. Modified Lilien Index
The statistic measures how differently each partner's flow grows relative to the country's total flow. If all partners grow at the same rate as the total, the index is zero. Higher values mean more reallocation across partners. This is the bridge from aggregate stability to bilateral reshuffling.

## Slide 11. Gravity Model
The gravity model compares between bloc pairs with within bloc pairs after the Ukraine war. Pair fixed effects absorb persistent bilateral frictions. Source time and destination time effects absorb country specific shocks. PPML is appropriate for trade and FDI because bilateral flows include many zeros and very skewed values.

## Slide 12. Table 1, Panel A: Wider Bloc, Trade and FDI
This slide reports the full trade and FDI columns for the wider bloc definition. The saturated columns are the main comparison. The trade coefficient of minus 0.1212 implies about 11.4 percent lower cross bloc trade relative to within bloc trade. The FDI coefficient of minus 0.1309 implies about 12.3 percent lower cross bloc announced FDI projects.

## Slide 13. Table 1, Panel A: Wider Bloc, Portfolio
The portfolio columns point in the same direction. The saturated coefficient is negative and statistically significant, implying lower cross bloc portfolio shares relative to within bloc holdings. This matters because the paper is not only a goods trade paper. It also documents geopolitical sorting in financial portfolios.

## Slide 14. Table 1, Panel A: Cold War Benchmark
The Cold War columns give scale. The between bloc coefficient is much larger than the modern estimate, and the nonaligned coefficient is also negative. This is why the title should be read as a benchmark question. The current episode shows early sorting, not Cold War scale separation.

## Slide 15. How to Interpret Table 1
For trade and FDI, the PPML coefficients are converted using exp beta minus one. The main result is not one isolated significant coefficient. It is the common direction across goods trade, FDI, and portfolio holdings. The Cold War coefficient is much larger, around a two thirds reduction in saturated cross bloc trade, which keeps the current estimates in perspective.

## Slide 16. Table 1, Panel B: Narrower Bloc, Trade and FDI
Panel B uses the narrower bloc classification. The estimates become less uniform, which is useful information rather than a problem to hide. The FDI result remains especially clear in the saturated specification. This suggests that investment location may react more sharply to geopolitical alignment than goods trade in the short run.

## Slide 17. Table 1, Panel B: Portfolio
The portfolio result under the narrower definition is negative and statistically significant in the saturated specification. The magnitude is larger than in Panel A. This means the narrower classification isolates a smaller set of politically distant pairs where financial portfolio sorting is more visible.

## Slide 18. Dynamics: Current Episode versus the Cold War
The dynamic figure reinforces the scale argument. The modern red line turns negative after the Ukraine war. The Cold War blue line becomes much more negative and remains low. The paper is showing the beginning of fragmentation, not a completed replay of the Cold War.

## Slide 19. Nonaligned Economies
This figure explains why today's world differs from the early Cold War. In the Cold War, trade involving nonaligned economies also weakened. In the current period, nonaligned trade does not collapse. That contrast prepares the connector country mechanism.

## Slide 20. Connector Countries: Trade
Panel A shows the core connector result. Nonaligned economies that gained US import share also tended to gain Chinese export share. This is consistent with indirect China US links through third countries rather than a clean break in supply chains.

## Slide 21. Connector Countries: FDI
The FDI version points in the same direction. Countries gaining US import share also gained Chinese outward FDI share. This makes the connector story more than a trade accounting pattern. It is linked to production location and firm investment decisions.

## Slide 22. Cold War Nonaligned Economies
The historical analogue is flat. Nonaligned economies in the early Cold War did not play the same bridge role. A plausible economic reason is that they were smaller, less open, and less integrated into global value chains.

## Slide 23. Economic Mechanism
The mechanism is rerouting, not clean decoupling. Direct links between rival blocs weaken in politically sensitive relationships. Some nonaligned countries gain market share and receive more Chinese FDI. Direct exposure falls, but indirect exposure can remain through third countries.

## Slide 24. Empirical Design
The method is well matched to the paper's question. PPML gravity handles zero heavy bilateral flows. Pair fixed effects remove persistent bilateral frictions. Country time effects absorb exporter and importer shocks. Using trade, FDI, and portfolio data makes the argument broader than a goods only story.

## Slide 25. Critical Assessment
The main limits are about scope and measurement. The paper measures reallocation but not welfare. It does not estimate price, productivity, or technology diffusion costs. FDI is based on announced projects, not realized capital. The bloc average may also hide China's asymmetric role as exporter, importer, and investor.

## Slide 26. Position in the Literature
The paper extends US China trade war evidence by making the question global and multi flow. It connects trade diversion, investment relocation, and portfolio sorting. The connector country result is the part that pushes the discussion beyond standard friend shoring.

## Slide 27. Bottom Line
My conclusion is simple. This is not yet a new Cold War. It is a measurable rewiring of globalization. Cross bloc flows are weakening, nonaligned countries matter more, and connector countries make policy harder because direct exposure can fall while indirect exposure remains.
